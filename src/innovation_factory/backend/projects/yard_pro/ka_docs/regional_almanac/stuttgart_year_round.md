# Stuttgart kettle — year-round garden almanac

Stuttgart sits in a steep-sided valley basin (the *Stuttgarter Kessel*)
that traps cold air at night and warm air during sunny days. The
practical consequences for gardeners are a slightly later last frost
than the surrounding region, a hotter, drier microclimate inside the
basin in midsummer, and a moderating effect from the surrounding
vineyards that buffers the worst extremes. This almanac is tuned to
those conditions; sites at higher elevations on the Filder plateau or
on the Alb edge will see slightly different timing.

## Last-frost window

Mid-April is the standard last-frost date for Stuttgart. After a cold
spring the window can stretch into the first week of May. The Saints'
Days frost (Eisheilige, 11-15 May) is a folk reference rather than a
hard guarantee — most years it does not bring frost in the basin
itself, but the surrounding plateau still sees light frost on those
nights. **Sensitive plants (tomatoes, basil, dahlias) should not be
moved out until after the Eisheilige in years when the prior two weeks
have been below average.**

## Month-by-month overview

- **January–February:** apple and pome-fruit winter prune (mid-Jan to
  mid-Mar). Lawn dormant.
- **March:** rose and panicle-hydrangea prune in the second half of the
  month. Lawn scarification and overseed once soil is workable.
  Slow-release tree fertilizer ring application to fruit trees.
- **April:** spring lawn fertilizer (NPK ~20-5-8). Pheromone traps out
  for box-tree moth by month's end. Tender plants stay protected until
  after the Eisheilige in cold years.
- **May:** hedge nesting law in effect (1 Mar – 30 Sep) — light shaping
  only, no hard cuts. Tomato and basil transplant after Eisheilige.
  Watch for the first wave of black spot and powdery mildew on roses.
- **June:** first beech-hedge trim in the last 10 days of the month.
  Apple fruit thinning. Lavender flowers begin to peak.
- **July:** lavender post-bloom shaping prune. Cherry summer prune
  immediately after harvest, in a dry week. Mid-month: midsummer balanced
  lawn fertilizer.
- **August:** second beech-hedge trim. Plum prune late in the month in
  dry weather. Watch for cherry fruit-fly trap catches.
- **September:** autumn potassium-heavy lawn fertilizer (NPK ~6-5-20).
  Continued boxwood-moth monitoring. After 30 Sep, hard hedge cutbacks
  are legally permitted again.
- **October–November:** rose autumn light tidy. Bulb planting (tulips,
  daffodils, alliums). Final lawn cut at 5 cm height.
- **December:** dormant; tool maintenance season. Sharpen blades,
  service mowers, store fuel-stabilised petrol.

## Water-stress threshold

A rough rule for the Stuttgart kettle: if the seven-day forecast is dry
and consistently above 28 °C, supplemental watering becomes worthwhile
for established beds. Below that, established planting needs no
additional water beyond what the rhythm above provides.

---

*This almanac is advisory only. Stuttgart microclimates vary from
basin to plateau; if your garden is on the Filder or near the Alb
edge, expect a frost window 1-2 weeks later than the basin and adjust
the schedule accordingly.*
