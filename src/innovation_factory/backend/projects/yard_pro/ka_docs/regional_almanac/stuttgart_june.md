# Stuttgart — June garden tasks

June is the month of first hedge trims, of mid-season watering decisions,
and of the natural June fruit drop on apples and plums. The weather in
the Stuttgart kettle moves rapidly toward summer-mode by mid-month: hot
afternoons interspersed with thunderstorms. Garden tasks shift to early
mornings.

## Weekend-by-weekend planner

### First weekend (5-7 June)

- The second generation of box-tree moth is approaching emergence;
  refresh pheromone capsules in traps if the manufacturer's interval
  has elapsed.
- Roses are at peak first flush. Deadhead spent flowers daily; this
  drives the second flush.
- Apple trees are setting fruit; watch for the natural June drop in
  the next 7-10 days.

### Second weekend (12-14 June)

- Lawn moves to summer-height mode: 5-6 cm cut. The taller blade
  shades its own root zone and is far more drought-resilient. Robotic
  mowers — adjust the height on the unit.
- Mid-June balanced lawn fertilizer (NPK ~15-5-15). Apply to a dry
  lawn and water in.
- Check tomato and basil plants weekly for the first signs of
  late-blight on tomatoes (yellow leaf edges, black stem lesions).
  Stake and prune side-shoots on indeterminate tomato varieties.

### Third weekend (19-21 June)

- **Apple fruit thinning window opens** — after natural June drop has
  finished. Thin each cluster to one fruit per spur. This sizes up
  the remaining fruit and reduces the alternate-bearing tendency.
- Roses approaching the end of the first flush; tidy spent flowers
  and continue base watering during dry weeks.
- First lavender flowers opening. Wait until the bloom is well past
  before doing the post-bloom shaping prune.

### Fourth weekend (26-28 June)

- **First beech-hedge trim window** (typical: last 10 days of June).
  Light shear, taking back most of the new growth but leaving 1-2 cm
  to encourage branching. Form-trim only — full cutbacks are not
  legal until October under §39 BNatSchG. Check for active bird nests
  before starting; skip a section if you find one.
- Box-tree moth caterpillar inspection: check the interior of every
  box plant for chewed leaves or webbing.

## Watering decisions

If the seven-day forecast crosses 28 °C consistently and shows no
rain, established beds benefit from a single deep weekly soak (20-25
litres / m² for lawns; saturating the drip-line for trees and shrubs).
Daily shallow watering is worse than no watering — it trains roots to
the surface where they cannot survive a real heat-wave.

## Watch-fors

- **Strawberry fruit ripening** — net early to prevent blackbird losses.
- **Cherry harvest** — early sweet-cherry varieties ripen in late June.
  Net plants before fruit shows colour; ripe cherries are gone in 48
  hours otherwise.
- **First powdery mildew on roses** — open canopy at next pruning;
  treat early.

---

*This planner is advisory only. June weather in the Stuttgart kettle
is highly variable; always check the local seven-day forecast before
heavy interventions like hedge trimming.*
