# Stuttgart — May garden tasks

May is the busiest month in the Stuttgart garden calendar. The
Eisheilige (Saints' Days, 11-15 May) marks the rough boundary after
which frost-tender plants can be moved outside, and the rest of the
month becomes a sequence of small, time-sensitive jobs: tomato
transplanting, first lawn cut at full season height, pheromone-trap
inspection for box-tree moth, watching for the first appearance of
disease pressure on roses and apple trees.

## Weekend-by-weekend planner

### First weekend (1-3 May)

- Apply spring lawn fertilizer if not already done (NPK ~20-5-8). Apply
  to a dry lawn, then water in.
- Scarify and overseed any bare patches in the lawn if you did not do
  this in late March. Surface temperature should now be reliably above
  10 °C.
- Final pruning window for panicle hydrangeas if your March prune was
  skipped.
- **Do NOT yet move out tomatoes, basil, dahlias** — Eisheilige risk
  still present.

### Second weekend (8-10 May, just before Eisheilige)

- Watch the forecast carefully. If a cold night is predicted, do nothing
  with tender plants; if the forecast is clear, prepare beds and
  containers for tender plants but keep the actual plants under cover.
- Check that pheromone traps for box-tree moth are deployed (should
  have gone out in late April).
- Spend time scouting roses for early black-spot lesions; remove any
  affected leaves and bin (do not compost).

### Third weekend (15-17 May, after Eisheilige)

- The Saints' Days have passed; this is the standard weekend to plant
  out tomatoes, basil, peppers, courgettes, dahlias, and any other
  frost-tender summer plants.
- Adjust robotic-mower schedule to summer rhythm (daily mowing at
  5-6 cm height).
- First-cut perennials (early-flowering varieties) start to show.

### Fourth weekend (22-24 May)

- Apple trees are setting fruit. Watch for the early signs of apple
  scab on the lower leaves; if airflow through the canopy is poor,
  consider a light summer thinning prune of green water-shoots.
- Lavender plants are budding; check for any wood-only stems and avoid
  trimming into them.
- Rhododendrons are coming into peak bloom; deadhead spent trusses
  to direct energy into new growth.

### Last weekend (29-31 May)

- Roses are about a week from peak bloom. Final structural-check pass:
  remove anything that crossed during early-spring growth.
- Beech hedge first trim window approaches — plan for late June, not
  yet.

## Watch-fors

- **Aphids on rose buds** — usually self-correcting; only intervene if
  shoots are visibly distorted.
- **Powdery mildew on rose foliage** — a sign of poor airflow; open the
  centre of the bush at next prune window.
- **Box-tree moth flight** — the first generation starts emerging late
  April / early May in the Stuttgart basin.

---

*This planner is advisory only. The Eisheilige is a folk reference, not
a guarantee; always check the local seven-day forecast before moving
out tender plants.*
