# Rose care, Stuttgart region

Roses do well in the Stuttgart kettle climate as long as a few
fundamentals are respected: full sun (at least six hours of direct
light), good air movement, and a deliberate March prune to set the
year's growth. The single most popular floribunda in southern Germany
remains 'Schneewittchen' (Snow White), and most of the rhythms below
are tuned for floribunda and shrub roses of that profile.

## Pruning

- **Main structural prune — mid- to late March.** Forsythia bloom is the
  traditional Stuttgart cue. Cut floribunda and shrub roses back to
  three or four strong outward-facing buds above ground (15-25 cm
  remaining stem). Remove dead, diseased, or rubbing canes. Aim for an
  open vase shape — air through the centre is the single biggest lever
  for reducing black-spot pressure.
- **Summer deadheading** — through July and August, cut off spent
  flowers just above the first five-leaflet leaf. This drives repeat
  flowering on remontant varieties.
- **Light autumn tidy — late October** — shorten the tallest canes by
  about a third to prevent wind-rock damage over winter. Avoid heavier
  cuts at this time.

## 'Schneewittchen' (Snow White) specifics

A vigorous, large-flowered floribunda introduced in 1958 by Kordes,
extremely popular in southern Germany. Notes:

- Reaches 100-140 cm tall when established, slightly taller in good
  soil — give it 80-100 cm spacing from neighbours.
- Very disease-tolerant by floribunda standards, but black spot can
  still appear in wet summers; a March pruning that opens the centre
  is the best preventative.
- Repeat-flowering rhythm: a main flush in late May / early June, a
  second flush in late July, then sporadic flowers into October.
- Lightly fragrant; petals shed cleanly. Excellent border or hedge
  plant.

## Fertilizer

A balanced rose fertilizer in two applications:

- **Early April**, immediately after the March prune. NPK around
  6-10-10 is a typical rose-specific blend.
- **Mid-June**, after the first flush. Same fertilizer.

Do not fertilize after mid-July — late nitrogen drives soft growth
that cannot harden off for winter.

## Pest and disease

- **Black spot** (*Diplocarpon rosae*) — the dominant rose disease in
  Stuttgart. Prevention: open canopy from spring prune; mulch
  generously; water at the base, never on the leaves; clear and bin
  (do not compost) fallen leaves in autumn. Treat early outbreaks
  with approved fungicides if pressure is high.
- **Aphids** — usually controlled by ladybirds and hoverflies. Only
  intervene if shoots are visibly distorted.
- **Powdery mildew** — see the separate playbook; usually a sign of
  poor airflow.

## Watering

Established roses on Stuttgart loam need supplemental water only
during dry, hot weeks. A deep weekly soak at the base — never on the
foliage — is the right rhythm.
