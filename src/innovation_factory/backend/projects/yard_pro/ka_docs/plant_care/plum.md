# Plum-tree care, Stuttgart region

Plums and damsons follow the same silver-leaf rule as cherries: do not
prune in winter. The standard pruning window for plums in the Stuttgart
region is **late summer**, between late July and early September, during
a dry week. A common mistake is to skip the prune entirely for several
years — plums respond by sending up dense water-shoots and producing
small, crowded fruit.

## Pruning

- **European plums** (*Prunus domestica* — including Hauszwetschge and
  Bühler Frühzwetschge) — light annual prune in late summer to remove
  diseased, broken, or rubbing branches and to keep the centre open for
  airflow. Aim to retain a balance of two- and three-year-old fruiting
  wood.
- **Damsons** (Mirabellen) — similar pattern; smaller framework so a
  lighter prune.
- Never cut more than a third of the canopy in a single season. Heavy
  cuts on plums also invite silver-leaf entry.
- Always cut just outside the branch collar. Use a clean, sharp pruning
  saw or bypass loppers; wipe blades with isopropyl alcohol between
  trees.

## Fertilizer

A March slow-release ring around the drip-line is enough for most
established plums. Excess nitrogen drives soft, watery shoots that
attract aphids and worsen brown-rot pressure on the developing fruit.

## Pest and disease watch

- **Plum-pocket / pocket-plum disease** (*Taphrina pruni*) — bag-shaped,
  pale fruits that ripen as hollow pockets. A late-winter dormant
  copper-fungicide spray (early March, before bud break) reduces
  pressure for the next season. Sanitation matters: pick up and bin all
  affected fruit; do not compost.
- **Plum-curculio / Pflaumenwickler** (*Cydia funebrana*) — the
  fruit-worm that causes early "June drop" of partially developed
  plums. Pheromone traps from late May give an early warning; an
  approved insecticide application at petal-fall is the standard
  intervention if pressure is high.
- **Aphids** — usually self-correcting via ladybird and hoverfly
  predation; only intervene if shoots are visibly distorted.

## Thinning

If the tree sets a heavy fruit load in June, thin to roughly one fruit
every 5-7 cm of branch. Unthinned trees produce small, soft fruit and
exhaust themselves, leading to biennial bearing.

## Watering

Established plums on Stuttgart loam rarely need supplemental water. A
deep weekly soak helps if the seven-day forecast is dry and over 28 °C
while fruit is sizing up.

---

*This guide is advisory only. Plum disease pressure depends heavily on
local rainfall and varietal susceptibility. If you see gummy oozing,
silvery leaves, or sudden branch dieback, photograph the affected area
and consult a local arborist.*
