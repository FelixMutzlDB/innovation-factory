# Lavender care, Stuttgart region

English lavender (*Lavandula angustifolia*) is the species most commonly
grown in Stuttgart gardens; lavandin (*L. × intermedia*) is the larger,
later-flowering hybrid sometimes used for hedging. Both prefer a
sheltered, sunny position with sharply drained, lean soil. Heavy clay
and rich, water-retentive soil are the two reliable ways to kill a
lavender plant in this region — far more reliably than any frost.

## Pruning rule (the only one that matters)

The single most important rule for keeping a lavender plant alive past
year three is: **never cut into old, woody stems.** Lavender does not
reliably re-sprout from bare wood. A light shaping prune after the
first bloom each year keeps the plant in cushion form for many years;
a hard prune into the woody base usually kills the plant.

## Pruning schedule

- **After first bloom — late July to early August** — shape the plant
  by removing the spent flower stalks and the top 1-2 cm of green
  foliage. Keep the cushion form. Never go below the lowest green
  shoots.
- **Optional second light prune — early September** — only if the plant
  put on substantial late growth. Same rule: stay in green wood only.
- **Spring shaping** — in late March, lightly shape if needed, again
  staying in green growth.

## Soil and watering

Lavender hates wet roots. If your soil is heavy Stuttgart loam, mix in
sharp sand or fine gravel when planting, and consider planting on a
slight mound so water drains away from the crown. Mulch with light
gravel or grit, not with bark or compost — organic mulch holds moisture
against the crown.

Established plants almost never need supplemental water. Even in a hot
summer, a light deep soak every two to three weeks is more than enough.
Daily watering kills lavender.

## Common Stuttgart varieties

- **Hidcote** — compact, deep-purple flowers, classic edging variety.
- **Munstead** — slightly looser habit, lighter purple-blue.
- **Grosso** (lavandin) — larger, late-flowering, often used for sweeping
  hedges or for harvest. More forgiving of trimming but still does not
  recover from cuts into old wood.

## Winter care

Lavender is hardy in the Stuttgart climate. A light covering of fir or
spruce branches in particularly cold weeks helps young plants but is
not generally necessary for established cushions.

---

*This guide is advisory only. The "never cut into old wood" rule is
the single most-misapplied piece of lavender advice; if you are
uncertain whether a stem is green or woody, leave it alone.*
