# Rhododendron care, Stuttgart region

Rhododendrons want what most Stuttgart gardens do not naturally offer:
acidic soil (pH 4.5-5.5), evenly moist but well-drained ground, and
dappled or filtered light. Plant them in heavy alkaline clay and they
will sulk for a year and then yellow off (iron chlorosis). Plant them
in deep shade and they will not flower. Plant them well, and they will
hold a slot in a Stuttgart garden for 30+ years with very little
intervention.

## Planting

- **Soil:** mix ericaceous (Rhododendronerde) into the planting hole at
  a 50/50 ratio with the existing soil. If your garden is on heavy
  alkaline loam, consider a raised planting bed where you fully
  control the soil.
- **Location:** east-facing or filtered-shade positions work best.
  Avoid afternoon sun on hot summer days, which scorches leaves.
- **Depth:** plant level with the surrounding soil — never deeper. The
  surface rooting system needs air.
- **Mulch:** pine-needle or bark mulch only, refreshed annually. The
  mulch breaks down to acidic compost over time.

## Watering

Rhododendrons are surface-rooted and dry out quickly. In the first two
seasons after planting, water deeply once a week regardless of
rainfall. Established plants need supplemental water only during dry,
hot weeks (more than seven days dry, above 28 °C); a deep soak around
the drip-line is the right rhythm. Rainwater is much preferred over
tap water in Stuttgart, which is alkaline and slightly hard — over
years, tap water shifts the soil pH out of the rhododendron's
comfort zone.

## Pruning

Rhododendrons rarely need pruning. Two situations call for it:

- **Light shaping prune** — immediately after flowering. Snap off spent
  flower trusses (just behind the flower, where the new growth shoots
  emerge) to direct energy into new growth.
- **Renewal prune of an overgrown plant** — in late March, before bud
  break. Rhododendrons re-sprout reliably from older wood, but recover
  slowly; expect one or two seasons before the plant looks
  reasonable, and three or four before it flowers properly again.

## Pests and diseases

- **Rhododendron leafhopper** — green, jumping; causes summer leaf
  damage and is the vector for bud-blast disease. Sticky traps in late
  summer help monitor.
- **Bud blast** — buds turn black and crusty; remove and bin (don't
  compost). Worsened by leafhopper presence.
- **Phytophthora root rot** — wilting despite moist soil. Almost always
  the result of poor drainage; the only real fix is to lift the plant
  and re-plant on better-drained soil.

## Iron chlorosis

Yellowing leaves with green veins is the classic symptom of iron
chlorosis, usually caused by soil pH drifting too high. Treat with an
iron chelate (Fe-EDDHA in alkaline soils) and a top-dressing of
ericaceous compost; correct the underlying pH over several seasons.

---

*This guide is advisory only. Rhododendron problems are almost always
soil and water problems disguised as leaf problems; if a plant is
yellowing or wilting, photograph the leaves and check the soil pH
before treating with anything.*
