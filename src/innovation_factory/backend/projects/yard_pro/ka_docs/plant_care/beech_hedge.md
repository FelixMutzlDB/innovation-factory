# Beech hedge care, Stuttgart region

A common-beech (*Fagus sylvatica*) hedge is a long-lived, formal hedge
that holds its bronze leaves through winter when it is sheared
correctly. It rewards two trims a year and a few seasons of patience.
The two-trim rhythm — once in late June, again in late August — is
what separates a dense, formal hedge from a leggy, see-through one.

## Trim windows

- **First trim — late June** (typical: 20-30 June in Stuttgart). The
  first growth flush has finished and the new shoots are still soft.
  Light shear, taking back most of the new growth but leaving 1-2 cm
  to encourage branching.
- **Second trim — late August** (typical: 20-30 August). The second
  flush has hardened off. A finishing shear here gives the dense,
  formal look that holds through autumn and winter. After this trim,
  the hedge holds its shape until the following June.

A single trim per year — typically late August — is also acceptable for
a less formal hedge, but the result is looser and inner branches go
bare faster.

## Bird-nesting protection (legal in Germany)

Note that German nature-conservation law (§39 BNatSchG) restricts
heavy hedge cutting between 1 March and 30 September. Form-trimming
(light shaping cuts) is allowed within that window, but a hard cutback
of an existing hedge must wait until October. The June and August
shears described above are form-trims, well within the allowed
operations — but always check for active nests before starting,
visually and by listening, and skip a section if you find one.

## Renewal of an overgrown hedge

A neglected beech hedge can be renovated by cutting back into older
wood in **October or November**, once nesting season has ended. Beech
re-sprouts reliably from older wood as long as you do not cut into
the absolute trunk in a single pass. Reduce in two seasons if the
hedge is more than 1.5 m too wide: cut one side back hard in year
one, the other side in year two.

## Tools and technique

A petrol or battery hedge trimmer with a 50-60 cm bar is the right
tool for residential beech hedges. Sharpen blades or replace blade
sets every 25-30 operating hours; a dull blade tears leaves rather
than cutting cleanly, leaving brown edges visible for weeks.

Always shape the hedge with a slight inward taper — the top narrower
than the bottom by 5-10 cm per side. A perfectly vertical hedge
shades its own base and goes bare from below.
