# Cherry-tree care, Stuttgart region

Cherry trees ask for one big behavioural change from people used to
apple-tree care: **never prune cherries in winter.** Cherries (and the
other stone fruits — plums, peaches, apricots) are susceptible to
silver-leaf disease (*Chondrostereum purpureum*), whose spores enter
fresh cuts during the cool, wet months. A winter prune on a sweet
cherry tree is the standard route to a silver-leaf infection. The
single rule that protects most home cherry trees in Stuttgart is:
*prune only after harvest, in a dry week, between late July and early
September.*

## Pruning

- **Sweet cherries** (Süßkirsche) — prune within two weeks of harvest.
  Remove diseased, broken, or crossing branches; thin the canopy
  lightly. Cuts heal quickly during warm dry weather; aim for cut
  surfaces dry within 24 hours.
- **Sour cherries** (Sauerkirsche) — same dry-summer window; sour cherries
  bear on one-year-old wood, so a slightly more aggressive renewal
  prune every 3-4 years keeps fruiting wood young.
- Always cut just outside the branch collar, never flush. A flush cut
  destroys the tree's own defence layer.

## Fertilizer

A balanced slow-release tree fertilizer ring application in early March
is enough for most established cherries on Stuttgart loam. Excess
nitrogen leads to thin, succulent shoots that are prone to aphid attack
and cracking.

## Pest and disease watch

- **Cherry fruit fly** (*Rhagoletis cerasi*) — yellow sticky traps from
  mid-May through harvest is the standard low-intervention measure.
- **Bacterial canker** — sudden gummy oozing on branches. Cut out the
  affected branch in dry summer weather and dispose of the wood; do
  not compost.
- **Silver-leaf** — silvery sheen on leaves of one branch first, then
  spreading. Cut the affected branch well below the visible damage,
  also in dry summer weather, and dispose.

## Bird protection

Stuttgart cherry harvest is short and concentrated. Lightweight bird
netting draped over the canopy a week before ripening is the single
most effective measure; cherry trees that are netted yield three to
four times what an unprotected tree yields.

## Watering

Established cherries rarely need supplemental water. Avoid watering
during the two weeks before harvest — wet conditions plus ripe fruit
equals split cherries.

---

*This guide is advisory only. Stone-fruit disease pressure is highly
local; if a tree shows oozing, dieback, or silvery leaves, photograph
the affected area and consult a local arborist or your dealer's
plant-care service.*
