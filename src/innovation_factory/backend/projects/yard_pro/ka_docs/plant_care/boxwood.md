# Boxwood care, Stuttgart region

Common box (*Buxus sempervirens*) is the classic clipped formal hedge
and topiary plant for German gardens. In the last decade it has come
under heavy pressure from two new threats: **box-tree moth**
(*Cydalima perspectalis*) and **boxwood blight** (*Cylindrocladium
buxicola*). A successful boxwood plan in Stuttgart today is structured
around prevention and early detection of these two threats, not around
the once-routine annual trim alone.

## Box-tree moth

The moth was introduced to Europe in the mid-2000s and is now endemic
across DE-BW. Caterpillars defoliate a plant from the inside out and
can strip a mature box within two to three weeks if left unmanaged.

**Pheromone trap rhythm for Stuttgart:**

- **Late April** — place pheromone traps before the first generation
  emerges. Refresh the pheromone capsule per manufacturer instructions
  (usually every 6-8 weeks).
- **Mid- to late June** — second generation emergence; refresh trap
  capsules.
- **Mid-August** — third generation; refresh capsules.

Pheromone traps are for monitoring, not control — they tell you when
to inspect. If you find caterpillars on the plant, intervene
immediately: a strong jet of water dislodges many, and approved
biological insecticides (Bacillus thuringiensis subspecies kurstaki)
applied to the underside of the foliage in the late afternoon control
the rest.

## Boxwood blight

Spreads by water splash and infected cuttings. Symptoms are circular
brown leaf spots, black streaking on stems, and rapid defoliation,
especially low on the plant. Wet, still air encourages the disease.

**Prevention:**

- Mulch with light gravel, not bark — bark holds moisture against the
  base of the plant.
- Trim in dry weather only; avoid trimming in muggy, still conditions.
- Sterilise hedge-trimmer blades with isopropyl alcohol between plants.
- Avoid overhead watering. If you must water, water at the base.

If you suspect blight: bag and bin the affected branches immediately
(do not compost), then inspect the whole plant. Repeated outbreaks
often mean the plant is in too damp a position to be saved.

## Trimming

A traditional box trimming rhythm is two cuts a year: a structural
shape in late May (Derby-Tag traditionally) and a finishing trim in
late August or early September. Always trim in dry weather. Don't
trim in strong sun — the freshly cut edges scorch.

## Alternatives

If you have lost boxwood and want a similar look without the disease
pressure, consider Japanese holly (*Ilex crenata*) or sweet box
(*Sarcococca confusa*) — both clip well and tolerate Stuttgart
conditions.

---

*This guide is advisory only. Box-tree moth and boxwood blight
pressure varies year to year and street to street; if you see leaf
spotting, sudden browning, or webbing on a boxwood, photograph the
affected area and intervene quickly.*
