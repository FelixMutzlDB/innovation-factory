# Hydrangea care, Stuttgart region

The pruning rule for hydrangeas depends entirely on which type you
grow. Confusing the types is the single most common reason home
gardeners report "my hydrangea did not flower this year." A
mophead-bigleaf cut back like a panicle will skip a whole flowering
season.

## Type-by-type pruning

| Type | Latin | Pruning window | Notes |
|------|-------|----------------|-------|
| Bigleaf (mophead, lacecap) | *H. macrophylla* | Mid-March, very light | Flowers on **old wood**. Only remove dead stems and spent flower heads, just above the first pair of healthy buds. |
| Endless Summer series | *H. macrophylla* re-blooming | Same as bigleaf | Flowers on both old AND new wood, so a light prune is more forgiving — but still no hard cut. |
| Panicle | *H. paniculata* (Limelight, Vanille Fraise) | Late March / early April | Flowers on **new wood**. Cut back to a strong framework 30-40 cm above ground; new shoots will flower. |
| Smooth | *H. arborescens* (Annabelle) | Late March / early April | Flowers on new wood. Cut back hard to the base if you want strong stems that support large flower heads. |
| Climbing | *H. petiolaris* | Just after flowering | Light shape only; this plant rarely needs major cuts. |

## Soil and water

Hydrangeas like rich, evenly moist soil with good organic content.
Stuttgart loam works well with the addition of compost at planting and
a mulch layer of bark or leaf mould refreshed each spring. Unlike
lavender, mophead and lacecap hydrangeas are thirsty plants; in a hot
dry week, a deep soak twice a week is appropriate, especially for
plants in full sun.

## Flower colour on bigleaf hydrangeas

Flower colour on bigleaf and Endless Summer hydrangeas is driven by
soil pH and aluminium availability:

- **Acidic soil (pH < 6)** — blues and purples dominate.
- **Neutral to alkaline soil (pH > 7)** — pinks and reds dominate.
- **Stuttgart soil tends toward slightly alkaline** — most bigleaf
  hydrangeas naturally flower pink here unless the soil has been
  amended. Aluminium-sulphate amendments shift toward blue but require
  re-application; ericaceous compost helps maintain acidity over time.

## Winter

Bigleaf and Endless Summer hydrangeas are best left with their dried
flower heads through winter; the spent heads protect the next year's
flower buds from frost damage. Cut back in mid-March, after the worst
of the cold has passed.

---

*This guide is advisory only. The most important point — match the
pruning window to the type — varies by plant; if you are uncertain
which type you have, leave the prune until after the first flowering
and identify the plant from its bloom shape and timing.*
