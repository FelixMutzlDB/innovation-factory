# Apple-tree care, Stuttgart region

Apple trees in the Stuttgart kettle climate do well when their pruning
and fertilizer windows track the local frost rhythm. The kettle's late
frost window typically closes by mid-April but can stretch into the
first week of May after a cold spring; this matters because tender new
growth that emerges before the last frost is the single biggest cause
of structural damage on young apple trees.

## Pruning windows

There are two pruning windows on a healthy apple tree:

- **Winter structural prune** — between mid-January and mid-March, before
  bud break. This is when you remove crossing branches, water-shoots,
  and any dead or diseased wood. Aim for an open, vase-shaped canopy so
  light reaches the inner branches and so air can move through after
  rain (the airflow alone reduces scab pressure noticeably).
- **Summer fruit-thinning prune** — late June through mid-July, after
  natural June drop has finished. Thin fruit clusters to one fruit per
  spur to size up the remaining fruit and to prevent the alternate-bearing
  cycle. A light summer prune of green shoots is also acceptable; do not
  remove more than 20% of the canopy in summer.

## Fertilizer

Most established apple trees benefit from a single slow-release tree
fertilizer application in early to mid-March, applied in a ring around
the drip-line, not at the trunk. Avoid high-nitrogen lawn fertilizer
near apple-tree drip lines — excess nitrogen drives leaf growth at the
expense of fruit and worsens scab susceptibility.

## Common Stuttgart varieties

- **Boskoop** — vigorous, cooking apple, very disease-tolerant. Pruning:
  open vase, biennial fruit-bearing tendency.
- **Cox Orange** — high quality eating apple but scab-prone; rigorous
  winter prune for airflow is non-optional.
- **Topaz** — scab-resistant modern variety, low-input choice for
  organic-leaning yards.

## Watering

Mature apple trees are deep-rooted and rarely need supplemental water
in a normal Stuttgart summer. After more than seven consecutive days of
dry, hot weather (> 28 °C), a deep weekly soak at the drip-line is
helpful for trees with set fruit. Shallow daily watering does more harm
than good.
