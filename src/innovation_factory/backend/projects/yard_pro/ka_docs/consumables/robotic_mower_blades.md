# Robotic-mower blades, trimmer-head grease, mower maintenance

The two consumables a homeowner with a robotic mower (and a hedge
trimmer / line trimmer) most often gets wrong are blade-replacement
interval and trimmer-head grease. Robotic-mower blades are cheap; a
worn set drops cut quality dramatically before most people notice.

## Robotic-mower blade-replacement interval

Most robotic-mower manufacturers specify blade replacement every
**6-8 weeks** during the mowing season for an average residential lawn
(roughly 200-500 m²). The blades on most modern robotic mowers are
small steel rectangles attached by a single screw to a pivoting disc;
they are designed to be replaced, not sharpened.

Symptoms of worn blades:

- Cut tips look frayed or split rather than cleanly cut. From a
  distance, the lawn looks slightly grey or "fuzzy" rather than fresh
  green.
- Mowing time per session increases noticeably as the mower passes the
  same area more often.
- Battery runtime per charge drops as the motor works harder against
  blunt blades.

Replacement is a five-minute job: power off, lift the mower, unscrew
each blade (typically 3-9 blades depending on model), install the new
blade, ensure free pivot, replace the screw. **Always** include the
small washer/spacer; without it the blade locks and stops swinging
when it strikes an obstacle, defeating the safety mechanism.

Keep a small stock of blades and screws (a season's worth — typically
3-4 sets per year for an average lawn). Most accidents during blade
replacement come from missing a single screw and re-using a damaged
one.

## Sharpening vs replacing

For traditional cylinder (reel) and rotary mowers, blades are
sharpened — usually once or twice a season — with a file or a bench
grinder. Robotic-mower blades are too small and too cheap to sharpen
economically; replace them.

## Trimmer-head grease

Petrol and battery line trimmers have a gearhead at the working end
that needs grease at a regular interval — typically every **25-30
operating hours**, or once per season for a homeowner. The grease is a
specific lithium-complex EP (extreme-pressure) grease; standard
all-purpose grease wears out quickly.

Symptoms of low grease:

- Whining or grinding sound from the head.
- Visible heat after 10-15 minutes of use.
- Reduced rotational speed.

Refill: locate the grease nipple on the gearhead (small flush fitting,
sometimes covered by a screw). Use a small grease gun or syringe to
inject 2-3 mL until clean grease appears at the housing seam, then
wipe excess.

## Hedge-trimmer blade care

Hedge-trimmer blades benefit from a light wipe of resin remover and a
thin film of oil **after each use**. Resin (sap) builds up on blades
during use; left in place, it accelerates wear and reduces cut
quality. Sharpening is needed every **25-30 operating hours** by a
service specialist; home sharpening is possible with a flat file but
must preserve the original blade angle.

## Mower deck cleaning

For rotary mowers, scrape grass clippings from the underside of the
deck after each use; built-up clippings reduce airflow and worsen cut
quality. For robotic mowers, lift the unit weekly during the mowing
season and rinse the underside with a hose; never use a pressure
washer.
