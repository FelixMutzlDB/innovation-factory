# 2-stroke fuel, alkylate fuel, and bar-and-chain oil

Petrol-powered outdoor tools — chainsaws, hedge trimmers, blowers, line
trimmers — fall into two fuel categories: 2-stroke (mixed fuel + oil)
and 4-stroke (separate fuel + oil reservoirs). The most common
mistake is running plain pump petrol in a 2-stroke engine; the second
most common is letting an ethanol-blended fuel sit in a tool over
winter.

## Mixing your own 2-stroke fuel

Most modern 2-stroke garden tools require a 50:1 fuel-to-oil mixture
(some older equipment was 25:1 or 40:1 — check the user manual). For
a 50:1 mix, that is 20 mL of 2-stroke oil per litre of fresh pump
petrol. Use only oils explicitly labelled "for 2-stroke air-cooled
engines" — JASO FB, FC, FD-grade oils are typical. Mix in a clean,
dedicated fuel canister, never in the tool's tank.

Fresh-mixed pump-petrol fuel is best used within **30 days**. After
that, ethanol-blended pump petrol absorbs moisture and the fuel
separates; the oil-phase is what reaches the engine and the
combustion drops off.

## Alkylate fuel (premixed)

Alkylate fuel is a pre-mixed, ethanol-free, low-aromatic petrol +
2-stroke-oil blend sold in 5 L cans (Aspen, Stihl MotoMix, Husqvarna
Premix, etc.). It costs noticeably more than pump petrol but has
several practical advantages:

- **Shelf life 3-5 years** sealed; 1 year once opened. This is the
  decisive advantage for tools that sit for months between uses (most
  homeowner chainsaws and hedge trimmers).
- **Reduced operator exposure** to benzene and other aromatic
  hydrocarbons. Significant if you operate the tool for more than an
  hour at a stretch.
- **Cleaner running**, fewer carbon deposits over years.

For a typical homeowner toolset (chainsaw + hedge trimmer + blower
running maybe 10-20 hours a year combined), alkylate fuel is almost
always the right choice. The price premium is real but small relative
to the cost of carburetor service after old-fuel damage.

## Bar-and-chain oil for chainsaws

Chainsaw bar oil is **not** engine oil. It is a sticky lubricant
designed to cling to the moving chain. Use only proper bar-and-chain
oil; vegetable oil and old engine oil are both bad choices (vegetable
oil polymerises and gums up; engine oil drips off and lubricates
poorly).

- **Top off every refuel** — bar oil and fuel typically empty at
  roughly the same rate. A correctly oiled chain leaves a faint mist
  of oil on the workpiece during a cut; a dry chain accelerates wear
  on both the bar and the chain.
- **Viscosity:** summer-grade bar oil for warm conditions (above ~10 °C);
  winter-grade for cold-weather use. The wrong viscosity in either
  direction underperforms.
- **Bio-degradable bar oils** are widely available and recommended for
  most homeowner use; they cost a little more but reduce
  ground-contact contamination.

## Storage between seasons

For tools that will sit for more than 30 days:

- Run the fuel system dry, or store with alkylate fuel only.
- Top off the bar-and-chain oil reservoir on chainsaws (the reservoir
  vents; an empty one builds condensation).
- Store dry, indoors, away from frost where possible.

*This guide is advisory only. Always follow the tool manufacturer's
specific recommendations for fuel ratio and oil grades. Old or
unknown fuel should be disposed of through municipal hazardous-waste
collection, never poured down a drain.*
