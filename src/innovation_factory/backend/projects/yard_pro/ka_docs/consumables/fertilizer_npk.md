# NPK fertilizer — selection, timing, and dosing

NPK fertilizers are labelled by their nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium
percentages, in that order. NPK 20-5-8 means 20% nitrogen, 5%
phosphorus pentoxide, 8% potassium oxide by weight. The right blend
depends on the plant and the season; using a single all-purpose
fertilizer year-round is rarely optimal.

## Lawn fertilizers — three-season rhythm

- **Spring (late March to early April):** NPK ~20-5-8 ("starter"). High
  nitrogen drives strong leaf growth out of winter dormancy. Typical
  application rate: 25-35 g/m². Apply to a dry lawn and water in.
- **Summer (mid-June):** NPK ~15-5-15. Balanced N + K for sustained
  colour through the heat and stress tolerance. 25 g/m².
- **Autumn (mid-September):** NPK ~6-5-20 ("autumn"). High potassium
  hardens the lawn for winter and improves frost tolerance. 25-30 g/m².
  Do not apply autumn fertilizer in spring — too little N for the
  spring growth flush.

## Slow-release tree fertilizer

For established apple, cherry, plum, and other fruit trees: a single
slow-release granular application in early to mid-March is enough for
most established trees. Typical rate: 80-100 g per m² of canopy
projection, applied in a ring around the drip-line, **never against the
trunk**. Roots take up the granules over 4-6 weeks; one application
covers the season.

## Rose fertilizer

- **Early April,** immediately after the structural prune: balanced
  rose-specific blend (e.g. NPK 6-10-10 or 7-10-10). Higher
  phosphorus and potassium support flowering.
- **Mid-June,** after the first flush: same blend.
- **Do not fertilize roses after mid-July.** Late N drives soft growth
  that cannot harden off for winter, and weakens the plant going into
  cold weather.

## Ericaceous (acid-loving) plants

Rhododendrons, azaleas, blueberries, and Japanese maples need an
ericaceous fertilizer — typically NPK around 8-5-10 with added iron
and magnesium. Standard alkaline-leaning lawn fertilizer drives iron
chlorosis (yellow leaves with green veins) on these plants over time.

## Application notes

- Always apply to a dry lawn or dry soil. Wet granules on wet foliage
  scorch the plant.
- Water in after application — the granules need moisture to dissolve.
- Wear gloves; ammonium nitrate granules are mildly caustic to broken
  skin.
- Store fertilizer dry, in original packaging, away from food. Most
  commercial NPK fertilizers will cake permanently if they get damp.

## Storage shelf life

Sealed and dry, granular NPK fertilizer keeps for 2-3 years without
loss of effectiveness. Once a bag is opened and re-sealed, plan to
use within one season — moisture from the air degrades nitrogen
content over months.
