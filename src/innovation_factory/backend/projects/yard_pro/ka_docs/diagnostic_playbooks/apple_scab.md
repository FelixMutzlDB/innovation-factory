# Diagnostic playbook — apple scab (and what it isn't)

Apple scab (*Venturia inaequalis*) is the dominant fungal disease of
apple trees in Stuttgart. The hallmark is olive-green to dark-brown,
circular lesions on the upper leaf surface, often with a slightly
velvety texture; on fruit, the lesions are corky scabs that crack open
as the fruit grows. Early identification is the difference between an
annoying but cosmetic year and a serious loss of fruit.

## Visual indicators

- Lesions are typically 3-10 mm across, roughly circular, **olive to
  dark brown** in colour with diffuse rather than sharp edges.
- Lesions appear first on the **lower, inner leaves** — the parts of
  the canopy with the least airflow.
- Heavily infected leaves yellow and drop early, sometimes by July.
- On fruit, scabs are corky, raised, dark brown, often radiating crack
  lines as the fruit expands underneath.

## Differential diagnosis — what apple scab is NOT

- **Powdery mildew** — white powdery coating on the upper leaf surface,
  often described as "dusted with flour." Distinct from scab's
  darker, embedded lesions. (See the powdery mildew playbook.)
- **Apple rust** — bright orange spots on upper leaf with hair-like
  yellow outgrowths on the underside (telia). Rare in Stuttgart
  unless juniper hosts are nearby.
- **Fire blight** — sudden wilt of branch tips with blackened,
  shepherd's-crook-shaped dieback. This is a bacterial disease, not a
  fungus, and is much more serious; immediate isolation and consult a
  specialist.
- **Healthy late-summer leaf** — mature apple leaves naturally develop
  some marginal browning by August; that is not scab.

## Treatment thresholds and response

| Level | Symptoms | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Trace | A few spotted leaves on lower canopy | Sanitation only. Pick affected leaves; bin (do not compost). Plan a winter prune to open the canopy. |
| Moderate | 10-30% of leaves spotted, some early drop | Sanitation + winter prune for airflow. Consider a dormant copper-fungicide application next February. |
| Heavy | > 30% of leaves affected, significant early drop, fruit visibly scabbed | Sanitation + winter prune + dormant copper next February. If pressure is consistent year-on-year, consider replacing with a scab-resistant variety (Topaz, Resi, Florina, Rebella) on the next planting. |

## Confidence cue for the vision model

Apple scab is one of the conditions most reliably identified from a
clear leaf-surface photograph; expect high model confidence on a
well-lit, focused, upper-leaf shot. **Low confidence** on a suspected
scab call should always prompt a "second opinion" suggestion — the
condition is similar in appearance to a few other foliar diseases,
and treatment choices differ.

## Stuttgart-specific notes

The kettle's still, humid summer afternoons increase scab pressure;
the prevention rhythm above is more important here than on
windier sites. The dormant copper spray (late February / early March)
is the single highest-impact preventative action you can take for an
already-affected tree.
